Analytics 101

Why R² Isn't Just a Club Name

5 min read · R-Squared Editorial Team

If you've ever run a regression, you've met R² before — it's the little statistic that quietly answers a big question: how much of what's happening in my data can my model actually explain?

The basic idea

R², or the coefficient of determination, tells you the proportion of variance in your dependent variable that's explained by your independent variables. An R² of 0.85 means your model accounts for 85% of the variation you're seeing — the rest is noise, or factors you haven't captured yet.

It's a number between 0 and 1 (sometimes expressed as a percentage), and it's one of the first things any analyst checks after fitting a model.

Why we picked the name

When we built this club, we wanted a name that captured something core to what analytics is about: explaining the unexplained, and being honest about how much you actually know versus how much is left to uncertainty.

  • R² rewards models that genuinely explain the data, not ones that just look complicated.
  • It's humble by design — a perfect score is rare, and rightly viewed with suspicion.
  • It's foundational — you'll meet it in your very first regression class, and again in every advanced model you build after.

A word of caution

A high R² doesn't automatically mean a good model — it's possible to inflate it by overfitting, adding irrelevant variables, or simply feeding the model more parameters than the data can support. Part of learning analytics is learning when to trust a number, and when to dig deeper.

That mindset — curious, but skeptical — is exactly what we try to build in every R-Squared member, whether through Vishlesh discussions, Ethos competitions, or our certification programs.
